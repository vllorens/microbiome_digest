Cholecystectomy‑associated gut microbiota dysbiosis promotes colorectal carcinogenesis: from epidemiological controversy to FXR‑centered mechanistic insights. This review, published in the Journal of the Egyptian National Cancer Institute, tackles a long‑standing debate: whether one of the world’s most common surgeries—gallbladder removal—altered colorectal cancer risk. The authors note that epidemiological studies have yielded conflicting results, leaving the relationship uncertain.

Central to this mechanistic synthesis is the farnesoid X receptor (FXR), a bile‑acid‑sensing nuclear receptor that regulates inflammation, epithelial proliferation, and DNA repair. The article argues that dysbiotic shifts after surgery may tilt FXR signaling toward a pro‑carcinogenic state, thereby providing a plausible biological bridge between the procedure and colorectal cancer development.
