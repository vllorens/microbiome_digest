They found that older participants exhibited a distinct pattern of nocturnal hydrogen excretion, with lower peak concentrations and a delayed rise compared to younger adults, and that these variations correlated with changes in sleep efficiency and slow‑wave activity. The sex‑specific analysis showed similar trends, though the magnitude differed between men and women. These results suggest that age‑related shifts in gut microbial fermentation and oxidative stress influence the hydrogen signal measured in exhaled breath during sleep.

The osmotic microbial fuel cell (OsMFC) simultaneously produces electricity, recovers water, and stabilizes and dewateres waste activated sludge, according to a study in Water Research indexed by PubMed. Researchers measured power output, sludge reduction, and tracked shifts in the microbial community alongside meta‑transcriptomic profiling to uncover how electron transfer is enhanced. The results show that the osmotic gradient drives ion flow that boosts extracellular electron transport, leading to higher current densities while the sludge becomes more stable and less voluminous. This dual‑function approach links bioelectrochemical performance with sludge treatment improvements.

Researchers showed that several understudied Bacillota (formerly Firmicutes) isolates from the human colon can depolymerise and ferment the plant‑cell‑wall polysaccharide β‑mannan. Using growth assays and enzyme activity tests, the team demonstrated that these bacteria possess mannase‑like activities that break down β‑mannans into fermentable sugars, leading to production of short‑chain fatty acids. This fills a gap in knowledge about how non‑starch polysaccharides influence gut microbes and highlights a metabolic capability previously attributed mainly to Bacteroidetes. The work appears in FEMS Microbiology Ecology, sourced via PubMed.

Patients scheduled for corneal transplantation harbor a distinct ocular surface microbiome, according to a single‑center case‑control study published in PubMed — Cornea. Researchers collected conjunctival swabs from 54 adults awaiting lamellar or penetrating keratoplasty and compared them with controls. The analysis revealed that microbial community composition differed significantly in those with corneal endothelial decompensation and in eyes with a history of prior surgery. These shifts suggest that the ocular surface microbiota may reflect disease state and surgical exposure, offering a potential biomarker for transplant risk. The work highlights the need to consider microbiome profiling in pre‑operative assessment of corneal graft candidates.
