A review in Environmental Pollution explains that heat or cold stress triggers gut dysbiosis, reducing beneficial bacteria such as Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium and lowering production of protective metabolites like short‑chain fatty acids. These microbial shifts are linked to inflammation, insulin resistance and other risk factors for heart disease and diabetes. The authors highlight temperature‑induced microbiome changes as a mechanistic pathway linking climatic extremes to metabolic disease, calling for further human studies to confirm the observed animal and in‑vitro findings. Source: PubMed — Environmental Pollution (Barking, Essex : 1987).

Multi‑omics analysis of Baobaoqu during storage uncovered the aging mechanisms that shape Wuliangye Baijiu quality. By integrating metatranscriptomics, metaproteomics and metabolomics with a multilayer perceptron neural network and SHAP explanations, researchers tracked how microbial gene expression, protein profiles and metabolite levels evolve as Baobaoqu matures. The approach identified key functional shifts—such as changes in carbohydrate metabolism and amino‑acid pathways—that correlate with higher‑grade Baobaoqu and, consequently, superior Baijiu flavor. The study demonstrates that combining layered omics data with machine‑learning interpretability can pinpoint the biochemical drivers of solid‑state fermentation aging. Oral hydrogel systems are emerging as a versatile approach to treat lower gastrointestinal disorders by directly interacting with the gut microbiota and epithelial barrier. A review in Materials Today Bio explains how these ingestable gels can be tuned to release therapeutics, modulate microbial metabolites, and reinforce mucosal integrity in conditions such as inflammatory bowel disease, irritable bowel syndrome, and colorectal cancer. The authors highlight a shift from disease‑centric formulations to microbiota‑guided designs, where hydrogel composition is selected based on specific microbial signatures and metabolic pathways. This strategy aims to improve precision, reduce systemic side effects, and harness the host‑microbe dialogue for sustained remission. Source: Materials Today Bio via PubMed.
