This randomized controlled trial is among the first to test early postoperative synbiotic supplementation for improving gastrointestinal symptoms and body composition after sleeve gastrectomy. Conducted in patients undergoing the procedure, the study compared a synbiotic regimen started soon after surgery with placebo, tracking postoperative GI complaints, microbiota shifts, and changes in weight or lean mass. By linking gut‑targeted nutrition to surgical recovery, the work addresses a gap in bariatric care where microbiome alterations often drive nausea, bloating, and malabsorption. The findings aim to clarify whether early synbiotics can ease these issues and support better nutritional outcomes. Source: PubMed — Clinical nutrition (Edinburgh, Scotland).

Chinese experts have issued the 2026 consensus on pediatric fecal microbiota transplantation, outlining 22 practical recommendations for its safe use in children. Published in Zhonghua er ke za zhi (Chinese Journal of Pediatrics) via PubMed, the statement was jointly developed by the Pediatric Infectious Disease Group of the Chinese Medical Association, its Parenteral and Enteral Nutrition Microbiota Collaboration Group, and the Maternal and Child Health Care Association’s Precision Medicine Pediatric Gut Microbiota and FMT Collaboration Group. It addresses four core areas—indication selection, donor screening and management, peri‑transplant care, and ethical standards—aiming to harmonize FMT practice across pediatric centers while acknowledging the unique physiology and disease patterns of young patients.

A scoping review published in Cancer Medicine (PubMed) concludes that higher dietary fiber intake is linked to better immunological responses to cancer immunotherapy, likely through favorable shifts in gut microbiota composition and enhanced anti‑tumor immunity. The authors synthesized existing preclinical and clinical studies, noting consistent associations between fiber‑rich diets, increased short‑chain fatty acid production, and improved checkpoint inhibitor efficacy across melanoma, lung, and renal carcinoma cohorts. While mechanistic pathways remain under investigation, the review highlights fiber as a modifiable factor that could augment immunotherapy outcomes and calls for prospective trials to define optimal dosing and timing.

Knocking out SCARB1, TYR, and TYRP1 in Oujiang Color common carp reshapes both appearance and internal physiology, according to a multi‑omics study published in PubMed’s Animal genetics journal. Loss of these genes produced visible changes in skin pigmentation, disrupted lipid and carotenoid metabolism, and altered the composition of the gut microbiome. Integrated transcriptomic, proteomic, and metabolomic data showed that the knockouts triggered compensatory pathways linked to oxidative stress and immune response, highlighting pleiotropic roles for SCARB1, TYR, and TYRP1 beyond color synthesis. The work demonstrates how targeted gene edits can illuminate connections between pigment genes, metabolic networks, and host‑microbial relationships in a teleost model.

Researchers report an oral nanoparticle system that delivers GSDMD‑N messenger RNA to colorectal tumor cells, inducing pyroptosis—a form of inflammatory cell death—while simultaneously reshaping the gut microbiota. By exploiting the tumor‑associated inflammatory microenvironment, the mRNA‑loaded particles trigger cancer‑specific necrosis, reduce pathogenic bacterial overgrowth, and lower polyketide synthase‑derived metabolites that promote CRC progression. In preclinical models, the approach suppressed tumor growth and improved survival without systemic toxicity. The work highlights a microbiota‑directed immunotherapy that couples gene‑based cell death with microbiome modulation. Source: PubMed — Small.

Individual short‑chain fatty acids produce distinct transcriptional reprogramming in colorectal cancer cells, showing that butyrate and propionate each drive unique gene‑expression programs rather than a uniform response. Using intestinal epithelial‑derived cancer lines, researchers treated cells with sodium butyrate or sodium propionate and performed early‑time‑point RNA‑seq to capture immediate transcriptional shifts. Butyrate strongly activated differentiation and apoptosis pathways, while propionate modulated metabolic and inflammatory networks. These findings reveal that the gut microbiota’s individual SCFAs can selectively shape host cell states, offering mechanistic insight into how microbial metabolites influence colorectal cancer biology. Source: PubMed — Journal of Biochemistry.

The study links chewing efficiency to gut‑derived metabolites, reporting that in a group of 20 healthy young adults (average age 27.6 ± 2.5 years) higher masticatory performance was associated with elevated fecal propionate levels. By measuring bite force, chewing speed and short‑chain fatty acid excretion, the cross‑sectional work provides novel evidence that mechanical oral processing may influence microbial metabolism in healthy humans. The findings suggest a potential pathway through which oral function could affect gut health, warranting further longitudinal investigation. Source: PubMed — Journal of Oral Biosciences.

A multi‑omics study published in Life Sciences (PubMed) reports that gut dysbiosis drives excess arachidonic acid, which promotes ferroptosis in the hippocampus and underlies central fatigue. Researchers integrated metagenomic, metabolomic and transcriptomic data from fatigued models, showing altered gut microbiota composition, elevated lipid peroxidation markers, and iron‑dependent cell death in hippocampal neurons. Blocking arachidonic acid metabolism or ferroptosis rescued fatigue‑like behavior, pointing to the gut‑brain lipid‑iron axis as a therapeutic target. The work highlights a mechanistic bridge between microbiome imbalance and brain‑specific oxidative death in central fatigue.
