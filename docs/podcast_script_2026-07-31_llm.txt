A new review in Brain Research Bulletin (PubMed) highlights that neuropsychiatric disorders are best understood as systemic conditions driven by dynamic gut‑brain‑immune interactions, with the microbiota‑gut‑brain axis (MGBA) acting as a central regulator of neurodevelopment, neural homeostasis and immune‑metabolic balance. The authors synthesize evidence across seven major psychiatric conditions, detailing microbial metabolites, vagal signaling, immune modulation and emerging therapeutic strategies such as probiotics, diet and fecal microbiota transplantation. Fructooligosaccharides (FOS) can reduce food‑allergy susceptibility in infants who received early‑life antibiotics by activating the indole‑3‑propionic acid‑AhR‑Nrf2 pathway, according to a multi‑omics prospective cohort study. The research, published in Food Research International (PubMed), shows that antibiotic‑induced dysbiosis lowers protective microbial metabolites, while FOS supplementation restores indole‑3‑propionic acid production, activates aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR) and Nrf2 signaling, and rebalances gut‑immune interactions. These mechanistic insights explain why FOS mitigates allergy risk and suggest a prebiotic strategy to correct immune defects caused by early antibiotic exposure.

Sodium butyrate, a short‑chain fatty acid produced by gut microbes, markedly lessens liver fibrosis by blocking aerobic glycolysis through the HDAC3/c‑Myc signaling pathway, according to a new study in the FASEB Journal indexed on PubMed. Researchers showed that the metabolite suppresses the Warburg‑like metabolic shift in activated hepatic stellate cells, lowering HDAC3 activity and downstream c‑Myc expression, which in turn curtails collagen deposition and fibrotic progression. The work clarifies how a microbiota‑derived compound can reprogram cellular metabolism to exert anti‑fibrotic effects, highlighting sodium butyrate as a promising therapeutic avenue for liver disease.

The formulation targets gut dysbiosis‑driven hepatic metabolic stress and downstream renal dysfunction by interrupting the bidirectional signaling crosstalk that drives T2DM complications. By simultaneously delivering curcumin’s anti‑inflammatory properties, gambogic acid’s targeting ability, and insulin’s glucose‑lowering action, the nanoparticles restore balance across the three organs.
