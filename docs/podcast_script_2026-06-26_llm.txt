A recent study in Annals of Neurology (PubMed) explores fecal microbiota transplant as a potential therapy for Parkinson’s disease, focusing on how altering gut microbes might influence the gut‑brain axis. The researchers report that FMT can induce short‑term shifts in microbial composition and associated neuroinflammatory markers, suggesting a mechanistic link between gut health and Parkinson’s pathology. The work underscores both the promise and the limitations of microbiome‑based interventions in neurodegenerative disease. Source: PubMed — Annals of neurology.

Prodromal gastrointestinal symptoms, α‑synuclein aggregates in enteric neurons, heightened intestinal inflammation, and compromised epithelial barrier integrity suggest that modulating the gut microbiome could influence neurodegeneration. Early clinical trials are assessing FMT’s safety and preliminary effects on motor and non‑motor symptoms, though robust efficacy data remain limited. This approach reflects a shift toward targeting the gut‑brain axis in Parkinson’s management. Source: PubMed — Journal of Parkinson's disease.

Researchers report that microbial DNA can be detected within human adipose tissue and that its abundance correlates with markers of insulin resistance and inflammation in obese individuals. Using quantitative PCR and sequencing on fat biopsies from patients with varying BMI, they found higher bacterial 16S rRNA signals in visceral fat of those with metabolic syndrome compared to lean controls. The study, published in The Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism, suggests that translocation of gut‑derived microbial fragments through a leaky intestine may directly impair adipose tissue function, offering a mechanistic link between gut dysbiosis and obesity‑related metabolic disease.

A novel victorivirus has been characterized from a human‑biting tick collected in Japan, marking the first report of this virus family in Amblyomma testudinarium. Researchers extracted total RNA from Vero cells exposed to tick homogenate and used metatranscriptomic sequencing to uncover the viral genome. The discovery adds to the growing catalogue of tick‑associated viruses and highlights how surveillance of arthropod vectors can reveal hidden viral diversity and inform evolutionary studies. The findings are reported in Microbiology and Immunology via PubMed, underscoring the importance of systematic vector screening for emerging pathogens.

Distinct intracanal microbiota and functional pathway patterns separate successful from failed regenerative endodontic treatments, according to an observational cohort study published in the International Endodontic Journal via PubMed. Researchers collected 196 samples at five time points—external tooth surfaces at T1 and T4, and root canals at T2, T3, and T5—from patients undergoing RET. Using V3‑V4 hypervariable region 16S rRNA sequencing, they profiled bacterial communities and inferred metabolic pathways. Success was linked to a higher abundance of health‑associated taxa and pathways involved in tissue repair, whereas failure showed enrichment of opportunistic anaerobes and inflammatory‑related functions. These microbial signatures may guide future RET protocols.

Intratumoral bacteria are now seen as integral, dynamic members of the tumor microenvironment, influencing every stage of cancer from initiation to treatment response. A recent review in PubMed‑indexed MedComm explains that these microbes can reach tumors through mucosal barrier breaks, blood or lymph spread, or local migration, and then shape immune remodeling, metastasis, and therapeutic efficacy. Highlighting both mechanistic links and clinical relevance, the article argues that targeting the intratumoral microbiota could become a new strategy to modulate tumor behavior and improve outcomes. Source: PubMed — MedComm

Glycan modifications are emerging as pivotal regulators of health and disease, according to a review in Trends in Biochemical Sciences (PubMed). The article highlights that glycans—polymeric carbohydrate chains decorating cell‑surface proteins and lipids—are highly dynamic and undergo diverse post‑synthetic changes such as O‑acetylation and sulfation. These alterations directly shape cell‑cell interactions, immune signaling, and the dialogue between host and microbiome, influencing processes ranging from inflammation to pathogen recognition. By detailing how specific glycan edits can switch functions on or off, the review underscores their potential as biomarkers and therapeutic targets in conditions spanning infection, autoimmunity, and cancer.

Natural microcosms have long been touted as ideal ecological model systems because they are widespread, easy to manipulate, and retain natural context. A review of 824 studies covering bromeliads, moss patches, nectar microbiomes, pitcher plants, rockpools and treeholes found that only 314 of those papers actually employed the microcosms as model systems, suggesting the promise remains only partially fulfilled.
