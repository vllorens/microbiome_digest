Cod‑skin collagen peptides can ease ulcerative colitis by reshaping the gut microbiome and dampening the NLRP3 inflammasome, according to a new study in Food Science & Nutrition. Researchers fed mice low‑molecular‑weight peptides derived from cod skin and observed reduced colonic inflammation, tighter intestinal barriers, and shifts in bacterial communities that lowered NLRP3‑driven cytokine release. The findings suggest these peptides act as a microbiota‑targeted enteral therapy, offering a mechanistic link between collagen supplementation and immune relief in inflammatory bowel disease. While human trials are pending, the animal data highlight a promising nutritional strategy for managing colitis.

Healthy gut microbiomes are vital for overall health and should be protected by embedding preservation into clinical decision‑making and policy, argue researchers in a PubMed‑indexed Anaerobe review. Natural compounds can reset the vicious epithelial‑immune loop that drives atopic dermatitis, according to a review in Molecular Immunology (PubMed). The authors argue that barrier loss, alarmin release, type‑2 cytokines, oxidative stress and microbial dysbiosis feed into shared signaling circuits, and that phytochemicals, vitamins and microbiome‑derived metabolites act not on isolated pathways but by modulating the overall mode of action—strengthening tight junctions, dampening alarmins, re‑balancing Th2 responses and reducing oxidative burden.
