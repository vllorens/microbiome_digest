D‑psicose, a rare sugar, reduced liver fat buildup and inflammation in mice with diet‑ and streptozotocin‑induced type 2 diabetes, according to a study in Food Science & Nutrition. The benefits coincided with shifts in gut‑microbiota composition, altered tryptophan‑derived metabolites, and activation of hepatic aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR) pathways. These linked changes suggest D‑psicose may ameliorate diabetes‑related liver injury through gut‑liver axis modulation. The work highlights a potential nutraceutical route for managing metabolic dysfunction in T2DM.

Oxygen exposure and diffusion are the primary formulation barriers when microencapsulating extremely oxygen‑sensitive (EOS) next‑generation probiotics, according to a recent review in the Journal of Controlled Release. The authors note that EOS strains, while promising as microbial biotherapeutics, cannot tolerate even trace oxygen, which compromises viability during processing and storage. Conventional techniques such as prilling‑based microencapsulation struggle to limit oxygen ingress, leading to rapid loss of activity. Overcoming these hurdles will require novel barrier materials or processing under strictly anaerobic conditions to ensure long‑term stability of oral probiotic therapies.
