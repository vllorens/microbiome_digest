Structural insights into xyloglucan recognition by an ABC transporter from a Gram‑positive, thermophilic bacterium reveal how the plant hemicellulose is captured for import. Using X‑ray crystallography, researchers determined the three‑dimensional architecture of the transporter’s substrate‑binding protein, showing a deep cleft that accommodates the β‑1,4‑glucan backbone of xyloglucan while forming specific contacts with its α‑1,6‑xylose side chains. This mechanism explains the bacterium’s ability to use xyloglucan as a growth substrate in habitats ranging from the gut microbiome to hot springs. The findings advance understanding of carbohydrate uptake in Gram‑positive microbes and highlight a potential target for engineering biomass‑degrading strains. Source: PubMed — The FEBS journal.
