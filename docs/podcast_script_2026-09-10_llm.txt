Chronic psychological stress harms male fertility by disrupting the gut‑testis axis, according to a new study in Brain, Behavior, and Immunity (PubMed). Researchers found that prolonged stress reshapes the gut microbiome, raising bacterial endotoxin levels that leak into the bloodstream and reach the testes. This endotoxin surge triggers inflammation and oxidative stress in the reproductive tract, lowering sperm count, motility and morphology. The work links psychosocial stress to measurable sperm quality decline through a gut‑derived inflammatory pathway, highlighting the microbiome as a potential target for mitigating stress‑related infertility.

Lactobacillus acidophilus strain LA3 can dampen neuroinflammation by acting on the gut‑brain axis, according to a new study in the Journal of Agricultural and Food Chemistry accessed via PubMed. Researchers exposed BV‑2 microglial cells to lipopolysaccharide to trigger inflammation and found that LA3 markedly reduced the release of pro‑inflammatory cytokines such as TNF‑α, IL‑1β and IL‑6. The probiotic also restored antioxidant enzyme activity and lowered oxidative stress markers in the cells. These results suggest that LA3 could be developed as a therapeutic adjunct for conditions where gut‑derived immune signals exacerbate brain inflammation.

Soluble, insoluble, and mixed rice bran dietary fibers each produce unique host‑microbiota‑metabolome signatures that modulate DSS‑induced colitis, according to a study in the Journal of Agricultural and Food Chemistry (PubMed). Researchers administered rice bran soluble fiber (RB‑SDF), insoluble fiber (RB‑IDF), and their mixture (RB‑MIX) at two doses to mice with chemically induced colitis. All three formulations reduced disease symptoms, histological damage, barrier loss, endotoxemia, and inflammation, but the fiber types drove different shifts in gut bacterial communities and distinct metabolic profiles, highlighting that fiber solubility dictates specific immunomodulatory mechanisms.

Researchers have isolated a natural gut symbiotic bacterium from mosquitoes, aiming to uncover the antiviral metabolites it produces that could curb arbovirus transmission. The study, published in PubMed‑indexed journal Virulence, notes that while mosquito gut microbiota is known to influence virus spread, the specific compounds and mechanisms behind this effect remain unclear. By isolating this commensal, the team sets the stage for identifying novel antibacterial or antiviral substances and elucidating how native bacteria modulate pathogenicity in the mosquito host. Source: PubMed — Virulence.

Researchers report the first integrated multi‑omics view of congenital central hypoventilation syndrome, showing that patients’ gut microbiota and fecal metabolome display a pro‑inflammatory signature. By comparing fecal DNA‑based microbial profiles and metabolite levels in a CCHS cohort with matched healthy controls, the team found shifts in bacterial taxa linked to inflammation and elevations in metabolites associated with immune activation. These alterations suggest that disrupted autonomic breathing control in CCHS may extend to gut homeostasis, fostering an inflammatory environment. The findings highlight a potential mechanistic link between PHOX2B‑related autonomic dysfunction and mucosal immunity. Source: PubMed — International journal of microbiology.

Obesity can boost cancer immunotherapy outcomes, contrary to its usual health risks, and this benefit stems from diet‑driven interactions with the gut microbiome rather than excess weight itself. Desharnais et al. demonstrated in mouse models that specific dietary components reshape gut microbes, enhancing checkpoint inhibitor responses. The study, reported in Cell Host & Microbe and indexed on PubMed, highlights a diet‑microbe synergy that explains the epidemiological link between obesity and improved immunotherapy efficacy. This insight suggests that modulating diet, not weight loss, could be leveraged to improve treatment success.

Researchers linked feed efficiency differences in lactating Holstein cows to variations in the rumen microbiome, digestion patterns, behavior and metabolic profiles. By calculating residual dry‑matter intake (RFI) as observed minus predicted intake, they examined 150 genotyped Holstein cows from three cohorts to identify which physiological and microbial traits most strongly predict low versus high RFI. The study, published in the Journal of Dairy Science via PubMed, shows that cows with lower RFI harbor distinct microbial communities and exhibit altered feeding behavior and nutrient utilization, offering mechanistic insight into divergent feed efficiency.

Extracellular vesicles released by lactic acid bacteria act as versatile postbiotics that link food‑technology innovations with intestinal health, according to a review in Critical Reviews in Food Science and Nutrition (PubMed). The authors highlight how these membrane‑bound particles, produced during fermentation, carry bioactive molecules such as enzymes, antimicrobial peptides and nucleic acids that can modulate gut microbiota, strengthen barrier function and exert immunomodulatory effects. By surviving processing stresses and remaining active in the gut, LAB‑derived vesicles offer a stable, inanimate alternative to live probiotics while retaining functional benefits. The review calls for standardized isolation methods and safety assessments to harness their potential in functional foods and therapeutic applications.

The International Scientific Association for Probiotics and Prebiotics (ISAPP) working group met at its 2025 annual meeting to pinpoint the molecular and cellular targets that shape the host’s response to biotics. Their report, published in Gut microbes via PubMed, synthesizes discussions on how probiotics, prebiotics, synbiotics and postbiotics interact with the immune system, barrier function and metabolic pathways. By highlighting consistent mechanisms across these interventions, the group aims to steer future studies toward more precise, host‑focused outcomes and to clarify why clinical results have varied. Source: PubMed — Gut microbes

Researchers mapping probiotic biogeography in the neonatal intestine found that specific spatial patterns of bacterial colonization correlate with reduced sepsis risk in preterm infants. By analysing gut samples from a cohort of low‑birth‑weight neonates, the study showed that uniform distribution of certain Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium strains along the intestinal tract was associated with lower inflammatory markers and fewer bloodstream infections, whereas patchy or absent colonization predicted higher sepsis incidence. The work highlights how probiotic formulation and placement within the gut, rather than mere presence, drive protective effects, pointing to a need for spatially targeted microbial therapies. Source: PubMed — mBio.
